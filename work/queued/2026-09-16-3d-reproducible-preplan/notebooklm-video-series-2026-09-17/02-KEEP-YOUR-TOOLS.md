# 02 — How does it fit our tools and method?

Audience: Teams evaluating fit.

Viewer outcome: Identify one useful process question within your existing workflow.

Target duration: 2–3 minutes. Spoken words: 268. Timing is a production target, not a verified recording duration.

## Narration

Can you use 3D Review with your existing checking tools? Yes. Can you use it with your translation method? Yes. Its questions focus on how your approach is being implemented and what people experience.

Your tools may help you inspect text, manage terminology, collaborate, or organize checking. Those contributions remain valuable. 3D Review brings structured observations from the translation team, community, and church into the conversation about the overall process.

Start with a practice you already use. Perhaps your team gathers community feedback. You can ask: who gets invited? How is that feedback understood? What happens when people disagree? How does it influence revision?

Those questions remain useful whether feedback arrives through a software tool, a facilitated discussion, or another method appropriate to your context.

Here is a hypothetical example. A team has a careful checking routine, but people are unsure what happens to the concerns they raise. The useful next inquiry is how feedback moves through the process. Simply confirming that a checking tool is installed would not answer that question.

Compatibility means using 3D Review alongside your approach. It does not promise that software automatically exchanges data, imports your project, or connects accounts. Any built-in integration needs its own verified documentation.

You also do not need to force every project into one identical sequence. Keep the language, medium, roles, and stage of the work in view when interpreting observations.

Choose one practice in your current workflow. Write down what you would like to understand about how it is working, then ask whose experience could help you answer. That is a practical starting point for assessing fit.
