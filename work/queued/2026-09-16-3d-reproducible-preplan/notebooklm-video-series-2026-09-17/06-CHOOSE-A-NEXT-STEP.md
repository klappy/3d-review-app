# 06 — How do we turn learning into a next step?

Audience: Team leaders, facilitators, and stakeholders.

Viewer outcome: Choose a bounded follow-up action and the evidence needed to learn from it.

Target duration: 2–3 minutes. Spoken words: 273. Timing is a production target, not a verified recording duration.

## Narration

An assessment becomes useful when it helps people decide what deserves attention next. That decision should stay connected to what people actually reported and to the limits of the evidence.

Begin with one question raised by the observations. Avoid trying to fix every possible issue at once, especially when the cause is still unclear.

Here is an illustrative example. People say they provide feedback but do not know what happens afterward. A reasonable first step could be to investigate how the team receives, discusses, and responds to feedback. That inquiry may reveal a communication gap, a process gap, or something else. Do not select the explanation before listening.

Bring the relevant people into the conversation. Check that you understood their observations accurately. Then agree on a bounded action, who will take responsibility, and when you will discuss what was learned. These are practical facilitation suggestions, not a claim about an automated feature in the app.

Decide what evidence would help you judge the action. If the aim is clearer feedback handling, for example, you might ask whether people can explain what happens after they raise a concern. Keep that example separate from any validated instrument or scoring rule.

When returning to an assessment later, preserve its context and versions. Changes in who participates or how questions are asked can affect comparisons. An apparent improvement does not, by itself, establish that your action caused it.

Close the loop by sharing what you learned, including what remains uncertain. The immediate outcome is one understandable next step and a way to learn from it, rather than a promise that a single assessment will solve every problem.
