# 03 — Whose perspective are we missing?

Audience: Facilitators and team leaders.

Viewer outcome: Identify relevant voices across all three lenses and one gap in representation.

Target duration: 2–3 minutes. Spoken words: 264. Timing is a production target, not a verified recording duration.

## Narration

A translation team knows things about its work that other people may never see. Community members and church participants know things about their experience that the team may never hear.

3D Review brings these perspectives together while preserving their differences.

Through the translation-team lens, people close to the work describe practices such as preparation, internal review, revision, and accountability. Different roles have different knowledge. One person's answer should not be treated as everyone's experience.

Through the community lens, people describe understanding, trust, and use. Their observations matter whether the translation is encountered in written, audio, or video-sign form. These are experiences of the translation, not just opinions about the team.

Through the church lens, participants and reviewers contribute observations about involvement, confidence, affirmation, and use. This perspective deserves to be heard in its own terms.

Consider an illustrative situation: the team describes strong review practices, community members report difficulty understanding, and church participants describe limited involvement. Those observations can coexist. They suggest questions to investigate, rather than a reason to average away the differences.

Before collecting responses, consider who is being invited and what they can reasonably know. A convenient group of respondents may leave an important perspective unheard. More responses from one group do not automatically fill a gap in another.

Write down the people or roles you hope to hear from under each of the three lenses. Then identify one voice that is currently missing and why it matters.

The outcome is a more thoughtful invitation plan: one that helps you learn from the people whose experience is relevant to the assessment.

Use three panels with role examples, not demographic stereotypes. Keep hypothetical observations labeled; no numeric scores or weighting.
