# 04 — How do we prepare people to participate?

Audience: Facilitators.

Viewer outcome: Prepare a clear invitation and an appropriate support plan.

Target duration: 2–3 minutes. Spoken words: 276. Timing is a production target, not a verified recording duration.

## Narration

Useful assessment begins before someone answers the first question. People need to understand what they are being asked to contribute and why their perspective matters.

Start by clarifying the context: the language, the assessment cycle, the relevant questionnaires, and the groups you hope to hear from. Explain that 3D Review gathers observations about the translation process and people's experience. It is not a test of the participant's ability to translate.

Use a simple invitation: what is this assessment about, why are you being invited, and where can you get help? Describe access and handling of responses according to the actual arrangements for your assessment. Do not promise anonymity or confidentiality that has not been established.

The accepted collection approach uses shared links so participants do not need a mandatory personal account or access code. People answer in separate response contexts; receiving a participant link does not grant access to other people's answers or staff functions.

A link alone does not remove every barrier. Consider language, literacy, familiarity with devices, and the practical setting. Source discussions identify facilitator training, paper collection, and support as important needs. Confirm what the current release and your local process actually support before promising a particular option.

Participants also need a clear submission outcome. If the interface reports uncertainty, follow the current documented recovery guidance rather than assuming either success or failure.

Before inviting a group, try the current journey with someone who represents your intended participants. Ask them to explain the purpose, find help, and recognize the submission outcome.

Your next step is a short invitation and a support plan that people can understand and that your team can actually deliver.
