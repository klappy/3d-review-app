# 05 — What can the evidence tell us?

Audience: Results readers and stakeholders.

Viewer outcome: Distinguish an observation, a gap in evidence, and a question requiring investigation.

Target duration: 2–3 minutes. Spoken words: 263. Timing is a production target, not a verified recording duration.

## Narration

When you read assessment evidence, begin with two questions: what are people telling us, and how much can this evidence support?

A low response and a missing response mean different things. An answer about context, an open comment, and an answer of “I don't know” also carry different meanings. Treating them all as numerical scores would lose useful information.

Look at whose perspectives are represented. A narrow set of responses may reveal something worth exploring, while still leaving important questions unanswered. Evidence coverage and the quality being assessed are separate considerations.

Keep the three lenses visible. If the translation team, community, and church describe different experiences, examine the difference before explaining it away.

Imagine a hypothetical pattern: team members describe a consistent review process, while some community respondents describe difficulty understanding the translation. You have observations that deserve inquiry. You do not yet have proof of the cause, a verdict on the team, or a direct diagnosis of errors in the text.

Context matters when comparing assessments. Check the language, assessment cycle, questionnaire version, and interpretation rules. A changed question can change what an answer means. A later profile may support discussion about change without proving what caused it.

Clear prose and attractive charts should help you understand evidence. They cannot make an unsupported conclusion true. Any new AI-generated interpretation needs review; it should not be confused with source-authored explanation.

Before making a decision, write three short statements: what we observed, what we still do not know, and what we should investigate next. That separation helps turn a report into a responsible conversation.
