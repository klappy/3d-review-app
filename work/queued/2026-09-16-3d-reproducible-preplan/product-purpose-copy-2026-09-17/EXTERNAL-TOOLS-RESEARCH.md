# Complementary checking tools — primary-source research

Observed September 17, 2026. This research describes the other tools from their own publishers. It does not prove a software integration with 3D Review. klappy's product direction establishes method/tool compatibility; historical Bee/cookbook evidence is being traced separately.

## translationCore

Primary source: https://translationcore.com/translationcore/ (unfoldingWord).

The publisher describes a desktop Bible-translation checking workspace. Its tools cover translation notes, key terms and original-language alignment. Teams review individual checks and track progress. The site describes importing projects in USFM. This supports presenting translationCore as a complementary place to inspect and improve the translation itself; it does not establish a 3D Review connector.

Additional primary workflow reference: https://tc-documentation.readthedocs.io/en/latest/translationNotes_tool.html . The notes workflow asks a reviewer to choose a verse, consult the relevant note and record the checked selection; difficult passages can prompt team discussion or consultation. Do not suggest checking software excludes people or collaboration.

## AQuA

Primary source: https://ai.sil.org/projects/AQuA (SIL Global AI & NLP).

SIL describes AQuA as an AI copilot that applies quality-assessment algorithms to a Bible translation and presents visualizations for the team to inspect. The purpose is to help people find potential problems in a draft and focus their expertise. This is support for direct text examination, rather than evidence of a 3D Review integration.

Primary explanatory article: https://sites.google.com/sil.org/siltranslationservices/home/ai-and-translation/mark-woodward — “Hudson Taylor and AQuA: AI in Translation Checking,” August 2026, Mark Woodward, SIL AI Capability Team.

The article describes visual and AI-assisted ways of raising questions about a draft, especially where language data is sparse. It emphasizes the language community's central role and treats AI observations as tentative, subject to human understanding. This reinforces complementarity: technical findings and human judgment belong together. It does not validate 3D Review's rubric or establish any causal claim about translation quality.

## Paratext as a third example

Primary source: https://paratext.org/ . Paratext describes support across study, drafting, revision, checking and publication, including collaborative work.

Primary process reference: https://manual.paratext.org/3.PP1/ . Its Assignments and Progress feature organizes tasks and checks, completion status and reporting. This is an important nuance: do not caricature existing checking tools as having no process support. The proposed distinction is 3D Review's specific three-lens assessment of implementation health, not an exclusive claim that only 3D Review considers teamwork or processes.

## Editorial application — explicitly synthesis, not vendor claim

Explain the two questions together: a checking tool can help a team inspect a passage or track required checks; 3D Review asks how well the people and practices behind the translation are functioning, bringing translation-team, community and church perspectives into the assessment. Keep the user's existing methods and checking tools in place. Describe any connection between their findings and a 3D Review conversation as an illustrative workflow, unless the cookbook independently establishes a specific feature.

“Compatible with any checking tool or Bible translation process” is the product's method-neutral positioning supplied by klappy. It is not a tested matrix of every vendor, a data-import guarantee, an API connection, an endorsement, or a reason to dispense with text checking. Avoid claims that healthy process automatically certifies correct verses, that tool adoption proves effective implementation, or that one lens can substitute for the others.

All third-party material above is paraphrased. No raw transcripts, copied marketing testimonials, graphics or long quotations were harvested. In downstream artifacts keep each vendor description brief and cite these direct primary pages; do not expand beyond the observed facts.
